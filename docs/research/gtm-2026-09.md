## 2. Trial length

Evidence:
- 14 days is the most common trial length (about 62% of products); 7 days and 30 days are about 14% each. When no card is taken up front, 14-day trials convert 8–12% better than 30-day ones. When a card is taken, 30-day trials do 5–8% better. Median conversion is about 14% without a card (opt-in) and about 44% with a card (opt-out). Source: growthspreeofficial.com/blogs/b2b-saas-trial-to-paid-conversion-rate-benchmarks-2026-by-trial-type-acv-length-credit-card (a secondary summary; treat as directional).
- A large randomized experiment found that trial length changes long-term conversion. Longer trials helped users who needed time to learn the product. Source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12217587/
- ChartMogul conversion report on free-to-paid rates: chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/
- Among competitors: Fresha and Acuity give 7 days, SimplyBook and Cal.com 14, Vagaro 30, Jane none. SimplyBook, Setmore, Calendly and Cal.com also have a free tier.

Recommendation: a **30-day opt-in trial, no card**. The clock starts on the first real day sheet printed, not on signup.
- Our value shows only after 1–2 weeks of real stays, which fits the "needs time to learn" group in the experiment above.
- Most Indian buyers have no card or will not put one on file, so an opt-out trial loses more signups than it gains in conversion.
- After day 30 the account becomes the free tier (section 4). It never locks up and data is never held hostage. This is a reverse trial: everything is unlocked first, then it drops to free.

## 4. Product-led growth and referral

Examples:
- **Dropbox**: each referral earns 500 MB (Basic, up to 16 GB) and the friend also gets 500 MB. Both sides are rewarded. Source: help.dropbox.com/storage-space/earn-space-referring-friends
- **Calendly**: every invitee sees a booking page that is "powered by Calendly", so each use is a demo to a new person. The free tier keeps the loop running (calendly.com/pricing).
- **Canva India**: local price of ₹4,000/yr (canva.com/en_in/pricing/), a generous free tier, and the Canva watermark and link on shared designs.
- **Zoho / Freshworks India playbook**: free-forever tiers with low INR pricing, self-serve signup, and inside sales by phone and WhatsApp. Known pattern; not re-verified here.
- **Practo**: the doctor-side software feeds the patient-facing listing, a two-sided network.
- Superhuman (waitlist plus white-glove onboarding), Loom (the shared video is the ad) and Jio (free first, habit, then price) are the same idea: the product carries itself to the next user.

Loops that fit us, ranked by effort:
1. **Branding on printed output.** Day sheets, discharge PDFs and guest links carry a small footer: "Made with Ruta · ruta.app/free". The day sheet hangs on a notice board seen by visiting doctors and therapists who rotate between centres. It costs nothing and every centre prints daily. Paid tier can remove it; the free tier cannot.
2. **Refer a centre: 3 free months each.** Both sides get it (Dropbox model), capped at 12 months. It is paid only when the referred centre pays for its first month, which blocks fake referrals.
3. **Visiting doctors.** Consultant vaidyas visit several centres. Give them a free read-only doctor link, and add a "Doctor: invite your other centre" link in it. The same referral reward goes to the centre.
4. **Associations and clusters.** Offer the founding price to AMAI members and Kerala Ayurveda centre clusters (district associations, Kerala Tourism's Ayurveda-classified centres). Do one demo in the members' WhatsApp group, with a 60-second video of a real day sheet. No ads.
5. **Guest discharge PDF.** Returning guests and their families see it. A line such as "Run a centre? Free for up to 5 guests" reaches future owners and managers.

Free tier rule: **free forever for up to 5 residents in house at once**, with all features and the branding kept on. A centre only pays once it is busy, so staying free doubles as a demo. It also helps the hosting sums in section 3, because free centres are tiny.

## 6. Minimal privacy notice

What Indian scheduling and clinic SaaS notices usually contain:
- What is collected (identity, contact, health and treatment notes).
- Purpose.
- The customer is the controller; the vendor processes data for them.
- Sub-processors and hosting location.
- Retention and deletion on request.
- Security measures.
- Grievance officer contact.
- Governing law.

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and the DPDP Rules 2025 require:
- A plain notice with an itemised list of data and purposes.
- Consent withdrawal that is as easy as giving consent.
- A contact for grievances.

Frugal outline (one page):
1. Who we are and a contact email (grievance officer = founder).
2. The centre is responsible for guest data; Ruta processes it on the centre's instructions.
3. Data we hold: staff and guest names, phone numbers, stay dates, therapy and schedule notes. No payment cards.
4. Where it lives: the host (for example Oracle Mumbai) and backups (Cloudflare R2). List these sub-processors.
5. Why: running the schedule and printing sheets. No ads, no selling, no AI training.
6. How long: while the account is active, plus 30 days after deletion; backups roll off in 30 days.
7. Your rights: access, correction, erasure, export (CSV/PDF) and withdrawal of consent. We reply within 30 days.
8. Security: HTTPS, encrypted backups, access limited to the founder.
9. Changes: 30 days' email notice.

Get a lawyer to review this before the first centre pays; it is a small cost. We did not fetch competitor notices in this pass.

### R2.1 Oracle Always Free: reliable enough for trials?

Oracle docs as of today (docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm):
- A1: 1,500 OCPU-hours and 9,000 GB-hours a month, which means **2 OCPU / 12 GB**. It was halved from 4/24 on 15 Jun 2026 without an announcement. Instances over the new limit were terminated from 18 Aug 2026 (infoq.com/news/2026/07/oracle-cloud-free-tier-limits/, bex.co/blog/2026/09/26/oracle-always-free-cut-free-tier-k8s-vs-hetzner).
- Storage: 200 GB boot and block in total, 47 GB minimum boot, 5 volume backups. Egress: 10 TB a month.
- Idle rule, quoted: "during a 7-day period ... CPU utilization for the 95th percentile is less than 20%; Network utilization is less than 20%; Memory utilization is less than 20% (applies to A1 shapes only)". Our Postgres plus Node on 12 GB will sit below 20% memory unless we size it deliberately.
- Capacity, quoted: "'out of host capacity' ... a temporary lack of Always Free shapes in your home region". No Mumbai or Hyderabad data was found. Workarounds are to retry and to try other ADs, but Mumbai and Hyderabad each have only one AD.
- PAYG, quoted: "Oracle doesn't charge for Always Free resources after you upgrade". Idle reclaim applies to *Always Free tenancies*, so PAYG accounts are exempt. A card is needed to sign up (verification hold).
- Termination stories: community.oracle.com/customerconnect/discussion/840035 (account terminated with no reason given) and discussion/974419 (instance and boot volume deleted).

**Verdict: OK for trials only after upgrading to PAYG on day one.** Mitigations:
- Set a budget alert at ₹1 so any charge is visible.
- Run a nightly `pg_dump` to Cloudflare R2, and use the Tunnel so no IP is exposed.
- Keep the whole compose file restorable on Hetzner in under an hour and rehearse it once.
- Never put paying centres on it.

### R2.3 Pricing page and self-serve signup: 10 rules

1. **Three columns at most**: Free / Centre / Centre+. Centre+ is the anchor and Centre is highlighted as "most popular" (Price Intelligently/Paddle, paddle.com/resources/saas-pricing-page-best-practices; not re-verified).
2. **Monthly/annual toggle, default monthly** in India, since small businesses resist upfront payment. Show annual as "2 months free", not as a percentage.
3. **INR first for Indian IPs, prices shown GST-inclusive** ("₹1,499/mo incl. GST"). Zoho India and Freshworks show "+ GST", but for a non-GST-registered micro-business the inclusive price is the honest one. USD for everyone else.
4. **Founding offer with a real counter**: "7 of 10 founding spots left, ₹999 locked for life". Real scarcity only, never fake.
5. **A live demo before signup**: a sandbox centre with seeded data that resets nightly, and a "Try the demo" CTA next to "Start free". Cal.com and Linear let visitors see the product before any commitment.
6. **Signup fields: email + centre name only.** Ask for phone, rooms and staff after the first day sheet has printed (Baymard: every extra field lowers completion; baymard.com/blog/checkout-flow-average-form-fields).
7. **Email magic link, no password.** Admins sign in rarely and forget passwords. Add a WhatsApp OTP later if emails bounce.
8. **Time-to-value under 5 minutes**: signup lands on a pre-filled sample day sheet they can print right away, then "replace with your rooms".
9. **No card for trial or free tier.** Ask for payment only when they pass 5 residents or the trial ends. Card-required trials convert better per trial but give far fewer trials (OpenView PLG benchmarks; not re-verified).
10. **An FAQ under the prices** covering data export, self-host option, cancel anytime and where data lives. Show the open-source/self-host link on the pricing page as a trust signal.

### R2.4 Billing

Facts:
- **Razorpay Subscriptions**: 2% + GST per debit, with no setup fee or AMC. UPI AutoPay, cards and e-NACH are supported, and webhooks such as `subscription.charged` and `subscription.halted` notify the app (razorpay.com/blog/cheapest-payment-gateway-for-recurring-billing-e-nach-upi-autopay-and-subscription/).
- **RBI E-Mandate Framework 2026** (issued 21 Apr 2026): recurring debits up to **₹15,000** need no OTP after an AFA registration. Customers get a pre-debit notice at least 24 hours before each debit (rocketpay.co.in/blog/rbi-e-mandate-recurring-payments-15000, businesstoday.in). Our ₹1,499/mo is well under the limit, but ₹14,990/yr is close to it and ₹29,990 would not fit.
- **Razorpay for a foreign entity**: the standard India account needs an Indian entity (PAN, GST or proprietor KYC). A Czech sole trader can only use **Import Flow**, which settles to a SWIFT account (razorpay.com/blog/international-businesses-can-now-seamlessly-accept-payments-from-india-with-razorpay/). Whether Import Flow supports subscriptions or UPI AutoPay was not confirmed.
- **Paddle Billing** (merchant of record) now supports **UPI AutoPay in INR with no India entity**. Recurring checkout must be under ₹15,000. Paddle's fee is about 5% + 50¢ (developer.paddle.com/concepts/payment-methods/upi/, updates.paddle.com). As merchant of record it handles GST and VAT, including Czech VAT.
- **Dodo Payments** (merchant of record): UPI and RuPay, INR, 4% + 40¢ (dodopayments.com/pricing, docs.dodopayments.com/features/payment-methods/india).
- **Stripe India**: invite-only for new accounts (not re-verified).
- **RevenueCat Web Billing**: supports only its own billing (on Stripe), Stripe Billing or Paddle, not Razorpay (revenuecat.com/docs/web/payment-integrations). It is overkill with no mobile app.
- **Chargebee/Cashfree**: both need an Indian entity. Chargebee is priced for larger volumes. Not re-verified.

**Recommendation: Paddle, with no billing engine of our own.**
- No India entity is needed, GST and VAT are handled, and UPI AutoPay works.
- On ₹999, a fee of about ₹90 is acceptable at founding scale.
- Dodo is the fallback if Paddle rejects the account.
- Move to Razorpay Subscriptions (2%) only if an Indian entity or Indian co-founder appears.
- Meanwhile the first 10 founding centres can pay yearly by UPI or bank transfer against a manual invoice.

Minimal data model: store only what Paddle's webhooks tell us.

`centre.billing = {provider, customer_id, subscription_id, status: trialing|active|past_due|canceled, plan: free|centre|founding, current_period_end}`

- Status drives one gate: if `past_due` lasts more than 14 days, the centre becomes read-only. Never delete data.
- No invoices, proration or tax tables live in our database.
