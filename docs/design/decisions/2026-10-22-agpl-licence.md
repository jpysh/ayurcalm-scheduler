# AGPL-3.0 with a commercial licence (#1054)

The code moves from MIT to AGPL-3.0, and stays complete: the planner, rules and day sheet are all public, never a front-end shell. A centre self-hosting for itself pays nothing and was not a customer anyway; what it cannot get free is setup, onsite support and hosting, which is what is sold.

The licence is aimed at the two real threats. An IT vendor reselling it, or a SaaS building on the code, must publish their changes; to keep them closed they buy a commercial licence, priced per deal ("contact us"), with a resale-partner margin preferred over a fight. Ideas cannot be protected by any licence; speed, Ayurveda depth and the pilot reference are the defence.

Contributions carry a licence grant (CONTRIBUTING.md) so the maintainer keeps the right to sell the commercial licence. The brand is trademarked as Ruta (India, classes 9 and 42) by the maintainer; renaming the app waits until the filing is in. Kept out of the repo: customer list, deal prices, pilot data, onboarding playbook.
